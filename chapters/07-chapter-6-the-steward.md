**p. 92**, "killing" rather than to what was asked"  
Paul Röttger, Hannah Rose Kirk, Bertie Vidgen, Giuseppe Attanasio, Federico Bianchi and Dirk Hovy, "XSTest: A Test Suite for Identifying Exaggerated Safety Behaviours in Large Language Models", NAACL 2024; arXiv 2308.01263, first posted 2 August 2023, v3 1 April 2024. **p. 99**, "the aircraft would handle like earlier 737s"  
US House Committee on Transportation and Infrastructure, "Final Committee Report: The Design, Development and Certification of the Boeing 737 MAX", September 2020 (GovInfo copy); the death toll from the committee's investigation page. MCAS's purpose, and Boeing's statement that it was not an anti-stall system, as summarised by Wikipedia, "Maneuvering Characteristics Augmentation System" (secondary). The two investigations: Komite Nasional Keselamatan Transportasi (Indonesia), final report on Lion Air Flight 610, October 2019, whose contributing factors include MCAS's reliance on a single angle-of-attack sensor and the absence of guidance on MCAS in the manuals and in crew training; and the Ethiopian Aircraft Accident Investigation Bureau, final report on Ethiopian Airlines Flight 302, 23 December 2022, which gave "repetitive and uncommanded airplane-nose-down inputs from the MCAS due to erroneous AOA input" as the most probable cause and described the crew's use of the stabiliser trim cutout switches, their failed attempts to trim by hand and MCAS's reactivation when electric trim was restored. The US National Transportation Safety Board published comments disputing the Ethiopian report's conclusion that MCAS alone made the aircraft unrecoverable, citing the crew's handling of thrust and trim. Lion Air: the report itself, and BBC News, 25 October 2019. Ethiopia and the NTSB: as summarised by Aviation International News and Wikipedia (secondary).
